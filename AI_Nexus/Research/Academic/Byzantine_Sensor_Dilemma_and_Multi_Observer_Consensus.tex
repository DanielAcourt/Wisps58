\begin{abstract}
Autonomous cyber-physical systems (CPS) operating in hazardous, uncertain, or deep-space environments face a fundamental duality of risk: cognitive drift in software-defined artificial intelligence agents and physical telemetry corruption in hardware sensors. Classical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT) addresses consensus over unreliable digital communication channels but fails to account for physical substrate decay, noise, or semantic agent hallucination. Conversely, traditional control theory relies heavily on Gaussian error assumptions (e.g., standard Kalman filtering), leaving safety-critical systems vulnerable to structural Byzantine lies where hardware streams physically plausible but empirically false data.

This master paper presents a deterministic, non-compensatory safety kernel framework designed to resolve the Cyber-Physical Byzantine Sensor Dilemma and Sensor Trust Recovery. Our primary contributions are: (1) The Epistemic Conditional Guarantee Principle ($\text{Formal Guarantee} \equiv \text{Theorem Validity Conditional on Explicit Assumptions}$); (2) The Observational Identifiability Limitation Theorem, proving that a single physical measurement channel without independent reference cannot distinguish environmental shifts from sensor bias ($x = h(s) + b$); (3) A $K \times N$ Tri-State Bit Matrix ($\mathcal{B} \in \{0, 1, \perp\}$) operating under a deterministic Void Safety Operator ($\mathcal{V}(\perp) \to 0$); (4) Theorem 1 (Byzantine Median Containment), proving that for $K = 2F + 1$ observers with at most $F$ Byzantine faults, the median estimator remains bounded within the honest observation envelope ($\min_{i \in H} x_i \le \operatorname{median}(x) \le \max_{i \in H} x_i$), and defining Effective Independent Observers ($K_{\text{effective}}$); (5) Proposition 1 (Bounded-Input Hysteresis) & Recovery Liveness, proving zero-chatter post-trip stability ($f_{\text{switch}} \equiv 0$); (6) Vessel Capability Envelopes ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{vessel}}$); and (7) Normalized Agent-Sensor Divergence ($\Psi_{\text{drift}}$) and Dimensionally Consistent Risk Velocity ($V_i = \frac{d D_i}{dt}$ with parameter $\beta \in [\text{time}]$) coupled directly to a Leontief Bottleneck Aggregation Function ($PSS = \min_i(D_i) \cdot \sum \alpha_i D_i$).

We critique recent literature claiming ``100\% mathematical certainty'' in safety-critical systems, demonstrating that physical sensor degradation invalidates pure geometric cages. We validate our framework through implementation in the Sovereign Engine (Unreal Engine 5 / C++ sub-system architecture), demonstrating deterministic fault isolation, rapid risk velocity throttling, zero-chatter hysteresis, and bounded trust recovery under adversarial sensor corruption and cognitive drift.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The Dual Nature of Cyber-Physical Byzantine Failures}
As autonomous agents transition from pure digital environments to physical embodiments---such as autonomous space probes, industrial robotics, and sovereign edge nodes---they encounter two distinct vectors of Byzantine corruption:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Byzantine Agent (Cognitive Drift):} Large Language Models (LLMs) and neural controllers experience semantic drift, context-window saturation, or localized hallucinations, leading them to issue structurally valid yet catastrophic operational directives.
    \item \textbf{The Byzantine Sensor (Physical Corruption):} Electro-mechanical hardware sensors (e.g., LiDAR arrays, thermocouple RTDs, pH meters, optical pyrometers) are subject to thermal degradation, electromagnetic interference, physical trauma, or adversarial spoofing, streaming structurally coherent but empirically false telemetry.
\end{enumerate}

In classical distributed systems~\cite{lamport1982byzantine}, BFT assumes network nodes may drop messages, delay packets, or act maliciously. However, in Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS), a sensor does not merely send arbitrary bits; it reports on physical state. A compromised sensor can maintain a plausible standard deviation while systematically driving an autonomous system past its safe operating envelope.

\subsection{Epistemic Distinction: Formal Guarantees vs. Physical Truth}
Recent software safety literature---most notably Simon (2026)---proposes geometric safety cages using Riemannian manifolds, zonotopes, and floating-point verification to claim ``100\% mathematical certainty'' and ``zero probability of failure'' in SIL 4 autonomous systems.

While mathematically elegant, such claims confuse formal controller verification with physical reality. We establish the \textbf{Conditional Guarantee Principle}:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{\text{Formal Guarantee} \equiv \text{Theorem Validity Conditional on Assumptions}}
\end{equation}

In physical reality, hardware substrates experience thermal degradation, cosmic radiation single-event upsets (SEUs), and environmental occlusions. No geometric cage constructed in pure software can guarantee physical safety if incoming telemetry feeding the manifold has been compromised by a Byzantine sensor. The Sovereign Framework bounds physical uncertainty under explicit conditions rather than claiming omniscient perfection.

\subsection{Observational Identifiability Limitation Theorem}
To replace informal physical metaphors, we state and prove the observational limitation of single-channel sensing:

\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Single-Channel Observational Identifiability Limitation]
Let $s \in \mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^p$ represent true physical environment state, $b \in \mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^p$ represent sensor bias/fault, and $h: \mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{R}^p$ represent a measurement function. Given a single physical observation channel $x = h(s) + b$ without an independent reference source, there exist distinct environment states $s_1 \neq s_2$ and fault states $b_1 \neq b_2$ such that the observations are mathematically indistinguishable ($x_1 = x_2$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Consider scalar measurement function $h(s) = s$. Let observation $x = 1200\text{ K}$. Hypothesis 1 represents true high temperature ($s_1 = 1200\text{ K}, b_1 = 0\text{ K}$). Hypothesis 2 represents nominal temperature with substrate bias ($s_2 = 300\text{ K}, b_2 = 900\text{ K}$). Since:
\begin{equation}
x_1 = h(1200) + 0 = 1200, \quad x_2 = h(300) + 900 = 1200
\end{equation}
both hypotheses yield identical observation $x = 1200$. Without independent reference information, $s$ and $b$ are unidentifiable from $x$ alone. Therefore, single-sensor self-recovery is epistemically impossible.
\end{IEEEproof}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Classical Byzantine Consensus}
Lamport, Shostak, and Pease~\cite{lamport1982byzantine} introduced Byzantine Fault Tolerance, proving that interactive consistency in a distributed network with $f$ faulty nodes requires $N \ge 3f + 1$ total nodes under unauthenticated messaging. Castro and Liskov~\cite{castro2002pbft} operationalized this via Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT). However, these protocols evaluate discrete digital message delivery rather than continuous physical variables.

\subsection{Resilient State Estimation \& Robust Filtering}
Kalman~\cite{kalman1960new} and extended Kalman filters (EKF) provide optimal state estimation under zero-mean Gaussian noise. However, under non-Gaussian persistent Byzantine corruption, standard Kalman gain matrices incorporate the corrupted state into the innovation step, causing catastrophic state drift. While robust filtering ($H_\infty$ control) attenuates outliers, traditional filtering remains compensatory---allowing high confidence in healthy sensors to average out subtle Byzantine corruption. Adversarially resilient estimation~\cite{pasqualetti2013attack, fawzi2014secure} addresses sensor attacks but assumes strict upper bounds on compromised channels.

\subsection{Runtime Verification \& Control Barrier Functions}
Runtime Verification (RV) under Spatio-Temporal Logic (STL) monitors system traces against formal specifications~\cite{deshmukh2017stl}. Simplex architectures~\cite{seto1998simplex} and Control Barrier Functions (CBFs)~\cite{ames2019cbf} enforce forward-invariant safety sets by overriding control actions. Our framework bridges RV, CBFs, and BFT by enforcing a \textbf{deterministic safety kernel over sensor validity itself}.

\subsection{Non-Compensatory Leontief Aggregation Function}
Standard multi-criteria evaluation functions use weighted additive averages, where high performance in one variable compensates for critical failure in another. In safety-critical CPS, high Administrative compliance ($D_A = 1.0$) cannot compensate for complete hydraulic failure ($D_T = 0.0$).

To enforce non-compensatory safety, the global \textbf{Provable Safety Status ($PSS$)} is governed by a \textbf{Leontief Bottleneck Aggregation Function}:

\begin{equation}
PSS = \min_{i \in \{P, S, T, A\}} (D_i) \cdot \left( \sum_{i \in \{P, S, T, A\}} \alpha_i D_i \right)
\end{equation}

where $\alpha_i > 0$ and $\sum \alpha_i = 1$. We explicitly adapt the economic Leontief production function over standard Boolean logic gates because it enables continuous degradation tracking ($D_i \in [0, 1]$) and proactive risk velocity dampening across all dimensions prior to triggering the hard non-compensatory $\min_i(D_i) \to 0$ trip.

\section{Mathematical Formalism of Multi-Observer Consensus}

\subsection{$K \times N$ Tri-State Bit Matrix \& Void Safety Operator}
Consider a physical property $\theta$ observed by a cluster of $N$ redundant sensor nodes across $K$ heterogeneous sensing modalities. Each sensor node $j \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ in modality $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ produces a raw reading $x_{kj} \in \mathbb{R}$ and a tri-state status bit $b_{kj} \in \mathcal{B}$:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B} \in \{1 \text{ (Nominal)}, 0 \text{ (Fault/Byzantine)}, \perp \text{ (Unvetted/Missing)}\}
\end{equation}

Stacking all $K$ sensing modality registers yields the global $K \times N$ Tri-State Bit Matrix ($\mathbf{B}$):

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{B}(t) = \begin{bmatrix}
b_{1,1} & b_{1,2} & \dots & b_{1,N} \\
b_{2,1} & b_{2,2} & \dots & b_{2,N} \\
\vdots & \dots & \ddots & \vdots \\
b_{K,1} & b_{K,2} & \dots & b_{K,N}
\end{bmatrix} \in \{0, 1, \perp\}^{K \times N}
\end{equation}

We define the non-compensatory \textbf{Void Safety Operator} $\mathcal{V}$:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{V}(b_{kj}) = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } b_{kj} = 1 \\ 0, & \text{if } b_{kj} = 0 \text{ or } b_{kj} = \perp \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Byzantine Median Containment}
We prove median containment under honest majority:

\begin{theorem}[Byzantine Median Containment]
Let $K = 2F + 1$ effective independent observers measure scalar physical variable $\theta$, where $H$ is the set of honest observers ($|H| \ge F + 1$), and $B$ is the set of Byzantine observers ($|B| \le F$). Assume all honest observations satisfy $x_i \in [\theta - \delta, \theta + \delta]$ for $\delta > 0$. Then the median consensus estimator $\bar{x}_{\text{median}} = \operatorname{median}(x_1, \dots, x_K)$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\min_{i \in H} x_i \le \bar{x}_{\text{median}} \le \max_{i \in H} x_i
\end{equation}
and consequently $|\bar{x}_{\text{median}} - \theta| \le \delta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Order observations $x_{(1)} \le \dots \le x_{(K)}$. The median is $x_{(F+1)}$. Since $|B| \le F$, at most $F$ values are strictly smaller than $\min_{i \in H} x_i$, and at most $F$ values are strictly larger than $\max_{i \in H} x_i$. Thus, $x_{(F+1)} \in [\min_{i \in H} x_i, \max_{i \in H} x_i]$.
\end{IEEEproof}

\section{Sensor Trust Recovery \& Sovereign Hysteresis}

When a sensor experiences a transient fault, re-admitting it instantly upon its first good reading causes rapid state oscillation ($\perp \leftrightarrow 1$), triggering the Flapping Trap. PSTA resolves trust recovery using three coupled mechanisms.

\subsection{Sliding-Window Handshake Auditor ($W_{\text{rec}}$)}
Let $H(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ denote packet handshake audit status at tick $t$. The sliding recovery window $W_{\text{rec}}$ of length $N$ evaluates sequential validity:

\begin{equation}
W_{\text{rec}}(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} H(t - k)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Sovereign Hysteresis Gap ($\Delta \tau_{\text{hys}}$)}
We define asymmetric thresholds: Fault Trip Threshold ($\tau_{\text{fail}}$) and Recovery Threshold ($\tau_{\text{recover}}$). The Hysteresis Gap is:

\begin{equation}
\Delta \tau_{\text{hys}} = \tau_{\text{recover}} - \tau_{\text{fail}} > 0
\end{equation}

The step-guard state $\theta_i(t)$ evolves as:

\begin{equation}
\theta_i(t) = \begin{cases} 0, & \text{if } D_i(t) < \tau_{\text{fail}, i} \lor \mathcal{B}_i = \perp \\ 1, & \text{if } \theta_i(t - \Delta t) = 0 \land D_i(t) \ge \tau_{\text{recover}, i} \land W_{\text{rec}}(t) \ge M \\ \theta_i(t - \Delta t), & \text{if } \tau_{\text{fail}, i} \le D_i(t) < \tau_{\text{recover}, i} \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Bounded-Input Hysteresis \& Recovery Liveness}

\newtheorem{proposition}{Proposition}
\begin{proposition}[Bounded-Input Flapping Boundedness]
Let a sensor signal $x(t) = \tau_{\text{fail}} + \epsilon \sin(\omega t)$ oscillate across the failure threshold $\tau_{\text{fail}}$ with noise amplitude $\epsilon < \frac{1}{2}\Delta \tau_{\text{hys}}$. Under Sovereign Non-Compensatory Hysteresis, following the single necessary initial fault trip transition ($\theta \to 0$ at $t = t_{\text{fault}}$), the steady-state post-trip state switching chatter frequency is strictly bounded:
\begin{equation}
f_{\text{switch}} \equiv 0 \quad (\forall t > t_{\text{fault}})
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Re-arm switching from $\theta = 0 \to 1$ requires $x(t) \ge \tau_{\text{recover}} = \tau_{\text{fail}} + \Delta \tau_{\text{hys}}$. Since peak signal $x_{\text{max}} = \tau_{\text{fail}} + \epsilon < \tau_{\text{fail}} + \Delta \tau_{\text{hys}}$, the recovery condition is never satisfied. Following the initial fault trip ($\theta \to 0$), the step-guard remains locked at $\theta = 0$ indefinitely. Thus, $f_{\text{switch}} \equiv 0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

To prevent permanent safe-hold lockup when a sensor returns to genuine health, we define **Recovery Liveness**:
\begin{equation}
\exists T_{\text{rec}} > 0 \text{ s.t. } \forall t \ge t_0 + T_{\text{rec}}, (D_i \ge \tau_{\text{recover}} \land W_{\text{rec}} \ge M) \implies \theta_i \to 1
\end{equation}

\section{Normalized State Spaces \& Dimensional Analysis}

\subsection{Normalized Agent-Sensor Divergence ($\Psi_{\text{drift}}$)}
Let cognitive agent intent vector $\vec{I} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and physical sensor telemetry vector $\vec{S} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be defined in a normalized engineering state space $\mathbb{R}^d$ with diagonal metric scaling matrix $\mathbf{W} = \operatorname{diag}(w_1, \dots, w_d)$:

\begin{equation}
\Psi_{\text{drift}} = \left( 1 - \frac{\vec{I}^T \mathbf{W} \vec{S}}{\|\vec{I}\|_{\mathbf{W}} \|\vec{S}\|_{\mathbf{W}}} \right) \cdot w_{\text{conflict}} + \frac{\|\vec{I} - \vec{S}\|_{\mathbf{W}}}{\|\vec{S}\|_{\mathbf{W}} + \epsilon}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dimensionally Consistent Risk Velocity ($V_i$)}
We define Risk Velocity $V_i$ as the numerical derivative of dimensionless health $D_i$:

\begin{equation}
V_i(t) = \frac{d D_i(t)}{dt} \quad \left[\text{units: } \frac{1}{\text{seconds}}\right]
\end{equation}

The dynamic health score is adjusted as:

\begin{equation}
D_i^{\text{dynamic}}(t) = D_i(t) + \beta \cdot \min(0, V_i(t))
\end{equation}

where parameter $\beta$ has explicit units of **seconds** ($[\beta] = \text{seconds}$). High-frequency noise is attenuated using a 5-point discrete moving-slope filter over sliding window $T_{\text{slope}}$.
