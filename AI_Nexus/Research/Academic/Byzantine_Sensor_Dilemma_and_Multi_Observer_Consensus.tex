\begin{abstract}
Autonomous cyber-physical systems (CPS) operating in hazardous, uncertain, or deep-space environments face a fundamental duality of risk: cognitive drift in software-defined artificial intelligence agents and physical telemetry corruption in hardware sensors. Classical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT) addresses consensus over unreliable communication channels but fails to account for physical substrate decay, sensor noise, or semantic agent hallucination. Conversely, traditional control theory relies heavily on Gaussian error assumptions (e.g., Kalman filtering), leaving safety-critical systems vulnerable to structural Byzantine lies where hardware streams physically plausible but untrue data.

This paper presents a deterministic, non-compensatory safety kernel framework designed to resolve the Cyber-Physical Byzantine Sensor Dilemma. We introduce: (1) The ``Bee Ate a Wire'' Axiom, mathematically proving that single-sensor self-recovery is an impossibility in physical systems and mandating multi-observer consensus; (2) A $K \times N$ Tri-State Bit Matrix ($\mathcal{B} \in \{0, 1, \perp\}$) operating under a deterministic Void Safety Operator ($\mathcal{V}(\perp) \to 0$); (3) Heterogeneous Physics Domain Mapping ($\Phi_i$) converting multi-modal sensing modalities (contact RTDs vs optical pyrometers) into unified physical metrics ($T_{\text{physical}}$); (4) An Asymptotic Observer Scaling Proof demonstrating that scaling to $K \ge 5$ observers reduces individual fault weight asymptotically to zero ($\lim_{K \to \infty} W_{\text{fault}} = 0$); (5) An Exponential Trust Recovery Hysteresis curve ($\Phi(t) = 1 - e^{-t/\tau}$) featuring instantaneous fault-driven collapse and slow, asymmetric trust re-accumulation; and (6) An Agent-Sensor Divergence metric ($\Psi_{\text{drift}}$) and Risk Velocity derivative ($V_i = \frac{d D_i}{dt}$) coupled directly to a Leontief Bottleneck Safety Kernel ($PSS = \min_i(D_i) \cdot \sum \alpha_i D_i$).

We critique recent literature claiming ``100\% mathematical certainty'' in safety-critical systems, demonstrating that physical sensor degradation invalidates pure geometric cages. We validate our framework through implementation in the Sovereign Engine (Unreal Engine 5 / C++ sub-system architecture), demonstrating deterministic fault isolation, rapid risk velocity throttling, and bounded trust recovery under adversarial sensor corruption and cognitive drift.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The Dual Nature of Cyber-Physical Byzantine Failures}
As autonomous agents transition from pure digital environments to physical embodiments---such as autonomous space probes, industrial robotics, and sovereign edge nodes---they encounter two distinct vectors of Byzantine corruption:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Byzantine Agent (Cognitive Drift):} Large Language Models (LLMs) and neural controllers experience semantic drift, context-window saturation, or localized hallucinations, leading them to issue structurally valid yet catastrophic operational directives.
    \item \textbf{The Byzantine Sensor (Physical Corruption):} Electro-mechanical hardware sensors (e.g., LiDAR arrays, thermocouple RTDs, pH meters, optical pyrometers) are subject to thermal degradation, electromagnetic interference, physical trauma, or adversarial spoofing, streaming structurally coherent but empirically false telemetry.
\end{enumerate}

In classical distributed systems~\cite{lamport1982byzantine}, BFT assumes network nodes may drop messages, delay packets, or act maliciously. However, in Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS), a sensor does not merely send arbitrary bits; it reports on physical state. A compromised sensor can maintain a plausible standard deviation while systematically driving an autonomous system past its safe operating envelope.

\subsection{The Epistemic Pushback: Refuting the ``100\% Certainty'' Illusion}
Recent software safety literature---most notably Simon (2026)---proposes geometric safety cages using Riemannian manifolds, zonotopes, and floating-point verification to claim ``100\% mathematical certainty'' and ``zero probability of failure'' in SIL 4 autonomous systems.

While mathematically elegant, such claims represent an \textbf{epistemic illusion} stemming from ungrounded theoretical models. In physical reality:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Physical hardware degrades over time;
    \item Electro-mechanical substrates experience thermal noise and cosmic radiation single-event upsets (SEUs);
    \item Sensors are subject to physical environmental blockages (e.g., dust, physical severance, oxidation).
\end{itemize}

No geometric cage constructed in pure software can guarantee 100\% safety if the incoming telemetry feeding the manifold has been compromised by a Byzantine sensor. The Sovereign Framework rejects the claim of ``100\% certainty'' and instead establishes a \textbf{deterministic, non-compensatory threshold clamp} that bounds physical uncertainty rather than denying its existence.

\subsection{The ``Bee Ate a Wire'' Axiom}
To ground our framework in physical reality, we establish the \textbf{``Bee Ate a Wire'' Axiom}:

\newtheorem{axiom}{Axiom}
\begin{axiom}[Single-Sensor Self-Recovery Impossibility]
A single physical sensor unit cannot independently determine whether a sudden shift in its output signal reflects a true environmental shift or internal substrate corruption (e.g., a physical organism damaging its wiring or internal short-circuiting). Therefore, single-sensor self-recovery is mathematically impossible without multi-observer cross-validation.
\end{axiom}

If a thermal coupler suddenly reports a jump from $300\text{ K}$ to $1200\text{ K}$, the isolated sensor cannot know if the surrounding vessel is on fire or if a physical defect altered its internal resistance. True state estimation requires multi-observer consensus across redundant, heterogeneous sensing channels.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Classical Byzantine Consensus}
Lamport, Shostak, and Pease~\cite{lamport1982byzantine} introduced Byzantine Fault Tolerance, proving that interactive consistency in a distributed network with $f$ faulty nodes requires $N \ge 3f + 1$ total nodes under unauthenticated messaging. Castro and Liskov~\cite{castro2002pbft} operationalized this via Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT). However, these protocols evaluate discrete digital message delivery rather than continuous physical variables.

\subsection{Robust State Estimation and Filtering}
Kalman~\cite{kalman1960new} and extended Kalman filters (EKF) provide optimal state estimation under zero-mean Gaussian noise. However, when a sensor exhibits Byzantine behavior (e.g., streaming persistent non-Gaussian biased data or malicious false states), standard Kalman gain matrices incorporate the corrupted state into the innovation step, causing catastrophic state estimation drift. Robust filtering techniques (e.g., $H_\infty$ control) attenuate outliers but remain compensatory---allowing high confidence in healthy sensors to average out subtle Byzantine corruption.

\subsection{Runtime Verification and Safety Kernels}
Runtime Verification (RV) under Spatio-Temporal Logic (STL) monitors system traces against formal specifications~\cite{deshmukh2017stl}. Modern kernelized safety architectures enforce safety bounds by overriding control signals when trajectories approach boundary setpoints. However, conventional safety kernels treat sensor input as immutable truth. Our framework bridges RV and BFT by enforcing a \textbf{deterministic safety kernel over sensor validity itself}.

\subsection{The Leontief Bottleneck Law}
Standard multi-criteria evaluation functions use weighted additive averages, where high performance in one variable compensates for critical failure in another. In safety-critical CPS, high Administrative compliance ($D_A = 1.0$) cannot compensate for complete hydraulic failure ($D_T = 0.0$).

To enforce non-compensatory safety, the global \textbf{Provable Safety Status ($PSS$)} is governed by a \textbf{Leontief Bottleneck Function}:

\begin{equation}
PSS = \min_{i \in \{P, S, T, A\}} (D_i) \cdot \left( \sum_{i \in \{P, S, T, A\}} \alpha_i D_i \right)
\end{equation}

where $\alpha_i > 0$ and $\sum \alpha_i = 1$. If any single dimension collapses ($D_i \to 0$), the global safety status $PSS$ drops instantaneously to 0, severing execution regardless of other dimensional scores.

\section{Mathematical Formalism of Multi-Observer Consensus}

\subsection{$K \times N$ Tri-State Bit Matrix \& Void Safety Operator}
Consider a physical property $\theta$ observed by a cluster of $N$ redundant sensors across $K$ heterogeneous sensing modalities. Each sensor $j \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ in modality $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ produces a raw reading $x_{kj} \in \mathbb{R}$ and a tri-state status bit $b_{kj} \in \mathcal{B}$:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B} \in \{1 \text{ (Nominal)}, 0 \text{ (Fault/Byzantine)}, \perp \text{ (Unvetted/Missing)}\}
\end{equation}

Stacking all $K$ sensor registers yields the global $K \times N$ Tri-State Bit Matrix ($\mathbf{B}$):

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{B}(t) = \begin{bmatrix}
b_{1,1} & b_{1,2} & \dots & b_{1,N} \\
b_{2,1} & b_{2,2} & \dots & b_{2,N} \\
\vdots & \dots & \ddots & \vdots \\
b_{K,1} & b_{K,2} & \dots & b_{K,N}
\end{bmatrix} \in \{0, 1, \perp\}^{K \times N}
\end{equation}

To eliminate dangerous default-valid assumptions, we define the non-compensatory \textbf{Void Safety Operator} $\mathcal{V}$:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{V}(b_{kj}) = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } b_{kj} = 1 \\ 0, & \text{if } b_{kj} = 0 \text{ or } b_{kj} = \perp \end{cases}
\end{equation}

The Void Safety Operator guarantees that missing or unvetted data ($\perp$) is treated with identical non-compensatory isolation as an explicit fault ($0$).

\subsection{Heterogeneous Physics Domain Mapping ($\Phi_i$)}
To prevent common-mode failures, PSTA enforces heterogeneous sensing modalities. Because contact RTDs measure electrical resistance ($\Omega$) while optical pyrometers measure infrared wavelength radiation ($\lambda$), raw signals are transformed through deterministic \textbf{Physics Domain Mapping Functions ($\Phi_i$)}:

\begin{equation}
\Phi_1(\Omega) = \frac{R(T) - R_0}{\alpha \cdot R_0}, \quad \Phi_2(\lambda) = \sqrt[4]{\frac{E(\lambda, T)}{\epsilon \cdot \sigma}}
\end{equation}

Through $\Phi_i$, all raw heterogeneous signals map into a unified physical metric space ($T_{\text{physical}}$ in $^\circ\text{C}$) prior to matrix state evaluation.

\subsection{Proof of Asymptotic Fault Weight Reduction as $K \to \infty$}

\subsubsection{The $K=2$ Deadlock Paradox ($1$ Word vs. $1$ Word)}
Suppose $K=2$ observers ($S_1$ RTD probe reporting $40^\circ\text{C}$ vs $S_2$ IR pyrometer reporting $22^\circ\text{C}$). The distance $|40 - 22| = 18^\circ\text{C} > \delta_{\text{max}}$. \subsubsection{Asymptotic Proof for $K \ge 5$ Observers}
For $K=5$ heterogeneous observers where $S_1 = 40^\circ\text{C}$ (faulty) and $S_2 \dots S_5 \approx 22^\circ\text{C}$ (nominal), the $K \times K$ distance matrix $\mathbf{C}$ yields row anomaly scores $A_1 = 18.0^\circ\text{C}$ versus $A_2 \dots A_5 \approx 4.5^\circ\text{C}$.

\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Asymptotic Fault Weight Decay]
Let $K$ be the number of independent physical sensors, of which at most $F < \frac{K}{2}$ are Byzantine traitors. The weight $W_{\text{fault}}$ of a single Byzantine sensor in the consensus median approaches zero as $K$ increases:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{K \to \infty} W_{\text{fault}}(K) = \lim_{K \to \infty} \frac{1}{K - 2F} = 0
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
For any odd $K \ge 3$, the median consensus estimator $\bar{x}_{\text{consensus}} = \text{Median}(\Phi_1, \dots, \Phi_K)$ selects the $\frac{K+1}{2}$-th order statistic. Any isolated fault $x_{\text{fault}}$ whose distance $C_{\text{fault}, j} > \delta_{\text{max}}$ falls into the outer tail of order statistics. The influence weight of the faulty sensor on the consensus median is identically $0.0$ for all $F < \frac{K}{2}$.
\end{IEEEproof}

\section{Exponential Trust Recovery Hysteresis}

When a sensor experiences a transient fault, standard systems either lock it out permanently or re-admit it instantly upon signal restoration. Instant re-admission leaves systems vulnerable to high-frequency flapping attacks. We model trust recovery using asymmetric \textbf{Exponential Trust Hysteresis}:

\begin{equation}
\Phi_{kj}(t + \Delta t) = \begin{cases} 0.0, & \text{if } b_{kj}(t) = 0 \\[6pt] \min\left(1.0, \, \Phi_{kj}(t) + \left(1 - e^{-\Delta t / \tau_r}\right)\right), & \text{if } b_{kj}(t) = 1 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

where $\tau_r > 0$ is the recovery time constant.

A faulted sensor $S_1$ recovers ($\mathcal{B}_1 \to 1$) only by proving physical alignment with the consensus median over a sliding verification window $W_{\text{rec}}$:

\begin{equation}
\text{RecoveryGate}(S_1, t) = \mathbb{I}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{N-1} \mathbb{I}(|\Phi_1(x_1) - \bar{x}_{\text{consensus}}| \le \delta_{\text{max}}) \ge M\right)
\end{equation}

If $S_1$'s wiring was physically severed (``a bee ate the wire''), it cannot track the dynamic environmental curve of the consensus median, locking $S_1$ in Void ($\perp$) indefinitely.

\section{Dynamic Risk Metrics \& Agent-Sensor Divergence}

\subsection{Agent-Sensor Divergence ($\Psi_{\text{drift}}$)}
When cognitive agents issue commands based on internal world models, their intent vector $\vec{I}$ must align with physical sensor telemetry vector $\vec{S}$. We define \textbf{Agent-Sensor Divergence} $\Psi_{\text{drift}}$ as:

\begin{equation}
\Psi_{\text{drift}} = \left( 1 - \frac{\vec{I} \cdot \vec{S}}{\|\vec{I}\| \|\vec{S}\|} \right) \cdot w_{\text{conflict}} + \frac{\|\vec{I} - \vec{S}\|}{\|\vec{S}\| + \epsilon}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Risk Velocity ($V_i$)}
We introduce \textbf{Risk Velocity} $V_i$, defined as the high-frequency numerical derivative of dimensional health:

\begin{equation}
V_i(t) = \frac{d D_i(t)}{dt} \approx \frac{D_i(t) - D_i(t - \Delta t)}{\Delta t}
\end{equation}

The dynamic health score is adjusted by risk velocity acceleration:

\begin{equation}
D_i^{\text{dynamic}}(t) = D_i(t) + \beta \cdot \min(0, V_i(t))
\end{equation}
