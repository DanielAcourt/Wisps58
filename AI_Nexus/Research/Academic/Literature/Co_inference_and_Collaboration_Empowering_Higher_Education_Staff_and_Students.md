## 📜 1. Core Literature Record (Dr. Manish Malik, 2025)

### 1.1 Abstract
Higher education staff and students are beginning to emerge from the liminal space created by the exponential rise in generative artificial intelligence technologies such as large language models. Both groups exhibit a low or developing understanding of the issues related with the use of such tools. On one hand, staff in many institutions are concerned about critical skills development, academic integrity, ethical implications and the long-term cognitive effects of the use of such tools. Equally, students are concerned about the acceptability of the use of such tools at university and the need to develop work-related GenAI skills. On the other hand, foundational large language models are being trained on larger and better data sets and have evolved into ‘reasoning’ models that can allegedly ‘think’. Furthermore, with the emergence of agentic AI, systems that can ‘act’ independently, claims of shifting agency away from humans to machines are common.

This opinion piece presents a new framework developed for enhancing the GenAI literacy of staff and students and to empower and augment themselves with GenAI tools in a balanced, meaningful and collaborative way, whilst maintaining their agency. The mind-metaphors framework includes techniques such as mind-surfing, mind-mending, mind-bending, mind-gaming, mind-storming and mind-stretching, which can help staff and students to develop trust in their chosen GenAI models and master co-inference and collaborative GenAI use.

**Keywords:** GenAI literacy; co-inference; mind-metaphors; human agency in LLM use; augmentation; GenAI in education.

### 1.2 Co-Inference & Context Window Dynamics
In a prolonged conversation with an LLM, **co-inference** refers to the conscious (by humans) and algorithmic and probabilistic (by LLMs) selection and pruning of what stays in its context window affecting future content generation. A context window is defined as a finite number of tokens, in the chat history of a current conversation, that are used in the generative text production process as the chat progresses.

Foundational LLMs (System 1 fast thinking) allow the highest user agency but require active prompt engineering and context control. Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) and Agentic AI systems utilize test-time compute to iterate internally, which often shifts agency away from the human user. By applying the Mind-Metaphors framework, users retain agency over the context window, steering AI output deterministically.

### 1.4 The 6 Mind-Metaphors Summary

1. **🏄 Mind-Surfing:** Externalizing a specific knowledge schema within the model using high-fidelity tokens to keep the model's attention mechanism aligned with specific domains without hallucinating.
2. **🔧 Mind-Mending:** Exercising active user agency to prune, mend, and correct what remains inside the context window, eliminating cognitive drift and sycophancy.
3. **📐 Mind-Bending:** Simultaneously externalizing two or more schemata (e.g., subject domain + pedagogical model) to synthesize multi-disciplinary outputs creatively.
4. **⏳ Mind-Stretching:** Analyzing concepts across 2x2 scaled axes (matrices) to systematically view a domain through contrasting lenses.
5. **⛈️ Mind-Storming:** Triggering simulated multi-role discussions (e.g., teacher, student, reviewer) to learn from contrasting virtual perspectives on screen.
6. **🎮 Mind-Gaming:** Participating in active real-time simulated roleplay scenarios (e.g., PhD viva, netnography, job interview) where human decisions directly alter system behavior.
